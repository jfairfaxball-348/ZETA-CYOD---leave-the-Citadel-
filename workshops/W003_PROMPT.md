## Objective
Decide what a *physical print-first* Choose Your Own Destiny novel could do structurally that feels intrinsically mathematical, is practical to navigate, and reinforces **agency, exploration and consequences**. The owner is interested in numbered paragraphs/pages in the manner of classic gamebooks (including optional dice rolled on printed page), and *random walks*. These are **PROPOSALS to critically examine**, not approved mechanics or permission to copy any copyrighted text, artwork, tables or distinctive rules expression.

### Explore one major design choice at a time
1. **Navigation vocabulary:** Compare page-jump and numbered-paragraph systems, or a deliberate hybrid; determine how illustrations and “turn to page 2” survive. Distinguish manuscript node IDs, paragraph labels, final page positions and route/flag tracking.
2. **Narrative graph:** What paths, cycles, detours, revisits, chokepoints, hubs and reconvergences belong in a three-act book? Could a controlled random walk create enjoyable exploration rather than uncontrollable backtracking? How do we retain a fast-forwardable shared Act I?
3. **Mathematical mechanics worth earning their space:** Consider random walks, simple dice or printed random-number alternatives; fair vs weighted branching, modular arithmetic/numbered jumps, graph connectivity, combinatorial choices, invariants, proofs by accumulating evidence, optional hidden patterns. Evaluate what is *fun*, comprehensible without specialist mathematical knowledge, and physically executable with a book/pencil/dice.
4. **Player agency:** Distinguish reader choices from random outcomes. Keep chance subordinate to meaning; avoid dice that repeatedly block necessary progress, softlocks, mandatory grinding, false choices, opaque tracking, or arithmetic homework.
5. **Print production and integrity:** Ability to use the printed work without electronic dependencies or spoilers; risk of recursive looping/softlocks, unreachable paragraphs, invalid references, accidental bypasses to Act II region choice, and unearned third-act endings. Discuss lightweight manual/automated graph checks only after design stabilises.
6. **Regional coherence:** Ask whether region-specific mathematics should affect *mechanics* as well as setting, without forcing three incompatible game systems or undermining the shared arc.
7. **Scope/reuse:** Gamebook-inspired dice/printed-page randomisation may be considered, but design ZETA's own expressions and rules. Avoid overbuilding a mini tabletop RPG unless there is a narrative reason.
