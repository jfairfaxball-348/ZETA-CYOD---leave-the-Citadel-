## Approved scene and choices

After P-0019 (*The Valley Below*) the apprentice enters the travellers' house. The woman with the nine-versus-eight Citadel delivery dispute from the earlier road route is playing chess with her husband. They warmly welcome the apprentice, provide food and conversation. Both are intelligent: the husband believes chess solvable in principle but out of practical reach because of immense possibility space; his wife believes a radically simpler, elegant formula may someday solve it without brute force. She describes hearsay about people from the Citadel whose life's work involves such questions. These are **character opinions**, not established chess results. The story must work for readers who followed the wall route and have not met the woman.

The exact three answers and their outcomes are approved:

1. **“Chess is technically solvable, but practically it is not.”** The husband admires the agreement, the wife enjoys an extended discussion, and the couple host the guest warmly for a while.
2. **“What is chess?”** The couple take the guest for an idiot and ignore them. The proprietor becomes suspicious of a lone stranger and tells the apprentice to leave **through the back door**.
3. **“I have already solved it.”** The couple request proof. The apprentice asks for paper and pen and writes a long, intricate formula they cannot understand. They keep the paper, the apprentice chats with them and eventually leaves **through the front door**. The formula is not certified as a chess solution: its truth is **open**.

Each of the three outcomes branches again. The six **provisional** second-choice prompts retained by the owner are: (1A) reveal exile / (1B) keep it private; (2A) return to the square / (2B) follow the rear passage; (3A) one final conversation / (3B) decline to explain further. These are **not completed prose**. Six routes converge pairwise into three joins, then into one shared Act I passage.
